# Data-Native Metaphors

Use this only when the idea is intrinsically about data work or when a familiar life fable would weaken semantic fidelity.

## When Data-Native Wins

- the claim is specifically about an outlier or anomaly
- the claim depends on a dashboard, KPI, SQL query, experiment, funnel, model, or pipeline
- the audience is a data team that recognizes the object instantly
- the data object can perform one concrete absurd action without labels

Do not add a dashboard merely to make a life fable look analytical. Select this lens only when data, evidence, measurement, or modeling is central to the source; the protagonist's behavior can carry the professional identity.

## Data Tensions

- signal vs. noise
- insight vs. dashboard theater
- decision vs. measurement
- outlier vs. average
- correlation vs. causation
- model confidence vs. human uncertainty
- clean spreadsheet vs. messy reality
- KPI worship vs. customer truth
- forecast vs. lived experience
- automation vs. judgment

## Strong Data-Native Patterns

### Outlier as witness

A real outlier sits in a witness chair while everyone questions the average.

- Use only for ignored anomalies, minority cases, or inconvenient observations.
- A red point is allowed because its literal role is an outlier.

### Dashboard as mirror

A dashboard reflects an uncomfortable reality while someone covers the mirror.

- Use for organizational self-deception or narrative versus evidence.
- Keep the dashboard abstract and unlabeled.

### KPI as ritual object

A manager waters, worships, or polishes a KPI gauge while the underlying system leaks.

- Use for metric worship or vanity metrics.
- Do not stack a gauge, chart, funnel, and alert in the same scene.

### SQL as excavation

An analyst excavates buried structure with a query-shaped tool.

- Use for uncovering hidden joins, lineage, or historical evidence.
- Keep the discovery concrete and recognizable.

### Pipeline as plumbing

A data pipeline leaks, clogs, or routes material to the wrong place.

- Use for data quality, missing events, or broken handoffs.
- Do not use a pipe merely to mean "the business."

### Correlation fishing

Someone fishes for causation in a pond full of correlations.

- Use only when the claim explicitly distinguishes correlation and causation.

## Red-Point Restriction

- Use a red data point only for a literal outlier, anomaly, selected observation, or alert.
- Do not use the same dot as a target, idea, input, insight, goal, or destination.
- If the point needs a sentence of explanation, replace it with a familiar object or action.
- The scene must remain understandable if the point becomes black.

## Failure Modes

- invented symbols that need a legend
- a generic person looking at a dashboard
- multiple data props competing for meaning
- an unlabeled red dot carrying the entire claim
- turning every abstract topic into a chart
- technical diagrams instead of editorial actions
- adding data decoration after a clearer fable already exists
