# Editorial Lenses

Use this after extracting the central contradiction. Choose the lens that makes the source visible with the fewest props and the least explanation.

## Selection Rule

1. Identify who experiences, causes, benefits from, or ignores the contradiction.
2. Place that person inside a familiar action.
3. Choose one prop that makes the mismatch physical.
4. Reject any lens that rewrites the source as a data, workplace, or technology problem when it is not one.

## Lens Catalog

### Everyday Life and Relationships

- Notice habits, expectations, avoidance, pride, intimacy, and self-deception.
- Use ordinary people, couples, families, neighbors, friends, or strangers.
- Prefer kitchens, doors, mirrors, umbrellas, chairs, gifts, queues, and household tools.
- Avoid turning a subtle emotional idea into a generic office joke.

### Workplace, Business, and Institutions

- Notice incentives, hierarchy, ritual, bureaucracy, customer distance, and organizational theater.
- Use workers, managers, customers, founders, officials, craftspeople, or consultants.
- Prefer meeting tables, ladders, stamps, assembly lines, counters, forms, and oversized tools.
- Avoid default dashboards unless measurement is the actual argument.

### Technology and AI

- Notice automation without judgment, scale without wisdom, human dependence, imitation, control, and unintended consequences.
- Use users, engineers, operators, researchers, or people caught inside a machine-made process.
- Prefer robots, switches, conveyor belts, charging cables, prompts as physical objects, and machines performing recognizably wrong tasks.
- Avoid literal UI screenshots and glowing science-fiction spectacle.

### Culture and Society

- Notice status, conformity, public performance, institutions, collective memory, and contradictions between ideals and behavior.
- Use citizens, audiences, artists, officials, tourists, neighbors, or crowds reduced to a few readable figures.
- Prefer monuments, stages, masks, frames, fences, podiums, and public signs without readable text.
- Avoid symbols that require local political knowledge unless the source supplies it.

### Knowledge, Education, and Creative Work

- Notice certainty, expertise, curiosity, imitation, teaching, interpretation, and the gap between knowing and doing.
- Use teachers, students, writers, editors, artists, experts, or apprentices.
- Prefer books, maps, telescopes, pencils, unfinished objects, recipes, and tools of practice.
- Avoid illustrating only "a person thinking"; show a consequential action.

### Data and Measurement

- Notice hidden assumptions, ignored denominators, causality traps, measurement bias, dashboard theater, and action gaps.
- Use analysts, scientists, managers, researchers, or decision-makers.
- Prefer scales, thermometers, rulers, samples, charts behaving as physical objects, and one recognizable analytical instrument.
- Read `data-metaphors.md` only for this lens.
- Avoid using a red dot unless the source is literally about an outlier, anomaly, or selected observation.

## Lens Test

Before developing a scene, confirm:

- The protagonist naturally belongs to the source.
- The chosen setting adds meaning rather than decoration.
- The action exposes the contradiction without a caption.
- A different lens would require more explanation or distort the claim.
